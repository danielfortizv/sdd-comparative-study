## Clarifications

### Session 2026-11-14

- Q: How should the system handle the initial state and persistence lifecycle of a recurring payment schedule when its periodic amount is greater than or equal to COP 1,000,000.00 and requires simulated MFA verification? (REC-03, REC-09, D-03) → A: Option A (Decision D-05): Persist in `PENDING_MFA` state (not `ACTIVE`) with a 300-second validity window; closing the modal leaves the schedule in `PENDING_MFA` and resumable via the UI; valid code `123456` transitions the schedule to `ACTIVE` (first run strictly after one interval per REC-04); expired challenges transition to `EXPIRED` without financial operations; no funds are debited during creation or confirmation; only `ACTIVE` schedules are eligible for automated execution.
- Q: In this simulated ACH environment without an external clearing network, what mechanism should determine whether an accepted pending ACH transfer transitions to 'settled' versus 'failed' (triggering the refund)? (ACH-06, ACH-07, A07, A08) → A: Option B (Decision D-06): Standard eligible `PENDING` ACH transfers settle autonomously via background processing without additional debits; simulation of `FAILED` ACH transfers is driven deterministically through an explicit non-public/test-only mechanism (never artificial recipient/bank magic strings); transition to `FAILED` refunds the debited amount once and excludes the amount from UTC daily limit consumption; `SETTLED` and `FAILED` are mutually exclusive terminal states.
- Q: When the customer updates dynamic limits, is the customer permitted to set a daily limit value that is lower than the amount already consumed within the current UTC day? (LIM-02, LIM-04, LIM-09) → A: Option A (Decision D-07): Yes; the updated daily limit takes effect immediately for all subsequent operations without altering prior completed transactions; remaining availability is calculated as `max(0, daily_limit - eligible_daily_consumption)` and never displayed as negative; new transfers and due recurring runs exceeding the newly reduced availability are rejected until an approved increase, a valid refund, or the next UTC midnight reset; limits persist across UTC boundaries without reverting.
- Q: In the beneficiary management interface, what lifecycle operations are supported for saved beneficiaries, and how does removing a beneficiary affect existing transaction history and active recurring schedules? (ACH-01, ACH-02, REC-11, A03) → A: Option A (Decision D-08): Saved beneficiaries support Create, List, and Delete operations only; banking details (name, bank, 9-digit routing, 4–17 digit account number) are immutable once created (updates require deleting and creating a new beneficiary); deletion removes the beneficiary from future selection while preserving historical records and active recurring schedules via immutable recipient snapshots; deletion causes no balance changes, transaction invalidations, or schedule cancellations.
- Q: When creating a recurring payment schedule with an explicit future start date, what format should the customer supply, and at what specific UTC time does the first execution become due? (REC-02, REC-04, A15, A16) → A: Option A (Decision D-09): Explicit start dates use calendar format `YYYY-MM-DD` and become due at 00:00:00 UTC on that date; the selected date must represent a strictly future execution instant (rejecting dates whose UTC midnight has already passed, including the current UTC date); if omitted, the first execution defaults to the creation timestamp plus exactly one full configured interval (+24 hours, +7 days, +30 days); subsequent executions advance by exactly one interval from the previous scheduled due date preserving the UTC execution instant; MFA confirmation does not shift the scheduled due date.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - User-Configurable Dynamic Limits (Priority: P1)

As an authenticated bank customer, I want to view and configure maximum transfer limits—both per-operation and daily cumulative limits denominated in Colombian Pesos (COP)—so that I can manage my transaction exposure and ensure outgoing transfers conform to my budgetary constraints.

**Why this priority**: Dynamic limits form the safety boundary for all fund movements (internal transfers, ACH transfers, and automatic recurring executions). They must be in place and verified before high-volume or automated operations can execute safely.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by accessing the dynamic limits interface, viewing default values (COP 5,000,000.00 per-operation and COP 10,000,000.00 daily), updating them, attempting transactions that violate either threshold (which are rejected before any step-up verification), and confirming that transactions exactly matching remaining thresholds are accepted.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the customer accesses the limits configuration, **When** the limits view loads, **Then** the interface clearly displays the configured per-transfer limit (default COP 5,000,000.00), configured daily cumulative limit (default COP 10,000,000.00), today's accumulated UTC consumption, and remaining daily availability (`max(0, daily_limit - eligible_daily_consumption)`).
2. **Given** effective limits of COP 5,000,000.00 per-transfer and COP 10,000,000.00 daily, **When** the customer attempts a transfer of COP 5,000,001.00, **Then** the system rejects the transaction immediately with a clear error indicating the per-transfer limit is exceeded, prior to any MFA challenge.
3. **Given** an effective daily limit of COP 1,000.00 and an adequate per-operation limit, **When** the customer submits successive valid transfers of 400.00, 300.00, and 300.00 within the same UTC day, **Then** all three transactions succeed (total 1,000.00), and a subsequent transfer of 10.00 on the same UTC day is rejected for exceeding the daily limit.
4. **Given** a customer updates the per-transfer limit to COP 2,000,000.00 and daily limit to COP 4,000,000.00, **When** saving the valid configuration, **Then** the new limits take effect immediately for all subsequent transactions and recurring payment executions.
5. **Given** a customer has already consumed COP 3,000,000.00 during the active UTC day, **When** the customer updates the daily limit to COP 2,000,000.00, **Then** the system accepts the update immediately, preserves all prior completed transactions, calculates remaining daily availability as COP 0.00 (never negative), rejects any subsequent transfers on that UTC day, and maintains the configured COP 2,000,000.00 limit across subsequent UTC midnight resets without reverting (D-07).
6. **Given** a customer attempts to submit a negative limit, zero limit, or an amount with more than two decimal places, **When** saving limits, **Then** the system rejects the modification and retains the previously active limits.
7. **Given** a sensitive transfer (>= COP 1,000,000.00) that exceeds the active per-transfer or daily limit, **When** submitted, **Then** limit validation fails before presenting an MFA prompt, and entering a valid MFA code cannot bypass an exceeded limit.

### User Story 2 - Simulated Third-Party ACH Transfers & Beneficiary Management (Priority: P1)

As an authenticated bank customer, I want to manage saved external beneficiaries (create, list, delete) with immutable banking details and send simulated third-party ACH transfers to external bank accounts—using either saved beneficiaries or ad hoc recipient details—with immediate source debit, transparent lifecycle status tracking, and single-refund handling upon failure, so that I can pay external parties reliably without contacting real clearing networks.

**Why this priority**: Interbank third-party payment is the primary new transaction type in Stage 2, introducing multi-party routing validation, beneficiary scoping, and asynchronous settlement lifecycle states (`pending`, `settled`, `failed`).

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating a saved beneficiary with a valid 9-digit routing number and valid account number, executing an ACH transfer under COP 1,000,000.00, verifying immediate source account debit and `pending` status, observing autonomous transition to `settled` without double debit, testing a failed ACH transfer to verify exactly-once refund, and verifying that deleting a saved beneficiary prevents future selection while preserving historical records and active recurring schedules via immutable recipient snapshots.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the customer is on the transfer interface, **When** initiating an ACH transfer, **Then** the UI clearly distinguishes ACH transfers from internal transfers and allows selecting a saved beneficiary or entering ad hoc recipient details (recipient name, bank name, 9-digit routing number, and 4–17 digit account number).
2. **Given** an ad hoc recipient entry, **When** the user types an invalid routing number (fewer than 9 digits, more than 9 digits, or non-numeric characters), **Then** the interface flags the error immediately during entry and disables submission until exactly 9 numeric digits are supplied.
3. **Given** an ad hoc recipient entry, **When** the account number is fewer than 4 digits or greater than 17 digits, **Then** the system flags an account format error and prevents submission.
4. **Given** an ACH transfer request with valid details and amount under COP 1,000,000.00 within available balance and limits, **When** submitted, **Then** the transfer is accepted in `pending` status, the source account balance is debited immediately once, and the movement appears as pending in transaction history.
5. **Given** an ACH transfer in `pending` status, **When** autonomous background settlement processing occurs, **Then** the transfer advances to `settled` with a recorded settlement timestamp, and no additional debit occurs on the source account (D-06).
6. **Given** an ACH transfer in `pending` status, **When** a simulated failure transition is triggered via the explicit test-only mechanism, **Then** the transfer status transitions to `failed` and the exact debited amount is refunded to the source account balance exactly once; subsequent calls cannot produce duplicate refunds or state regressions, and the refunded amount is excluded from daily limit consumption (D-06).
7. **Given** an ACH transfer amount greater than or equal to COP 1,000,000.00, **When** submitted with valid limits and balance, **Then** the system presents the inherited MFA verification challenge (code `123456`), and only debits the source account upon valid code submission.
8. **Given** the customer manages saved beneficiaries, **When** viewing the beneficiary management interface, **Then** the user can create new beneficiaries, view a list of all existing beneficiaries belonging to them, and delete existing beneficiaries; details cannot be edited in place (changes require deleting and re-creating) (D-08).
9. **Given** an existing saved beneficiary is deleted by the customer, **When** the deletion occurs, **Then** the beneficiary is removed from future transfer selection, but previously recorded ACH transfers and existing recurring schedules retain their immutable recipient snapshots and continue without error, without modifying balances or transaction history (D-08).

### User Story 3 - Scheduled Recurring Payments Lifecycle & Automatic Execution (Priority: P2)

As an authenticated bank customer, I want to create, inspect, pause, resume, and cancel automated recurring payment schedules for both internal and ACH transfers with configurable frequencies (daily, weekly, monthly) and deterministic UTC start dates, so that recurring financial commitments are fulfilled automatically when due without requiring repetitive manual entry.

**Why this priority**: Recurring payments introduce autonomous time-based execution and state lifecycle management (`PENDING_MFA`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED`), requiring automated background evaluation while maintaining strict limit and balance checks.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating an active recurring internal and ACH payment schedule, verifying the default first due date calculation (+1 day / 24h for daily, +7 days for weekly, strictly +30 days for monthly) and explicit future start dates (`YYYY-MM-DD` at 00:00:00 UTC), allowing autonomous execution when due to confirm balance debit and movement recording, pausing and resuming the schedule, and verifying that cancelled schedules can never be reactivated or re-executed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the customer creates a recurring payment schedule, **When** selecting frequency, **Then** available options are Daily (+1 day / 24 hours), Weekly (+7 days), and Monthly (strictly +30 days, not variable calendar months) (REC-02, D-09).
2. **Given** a recurring payment schedule created without an explicit future start date, **When** created, **Then** its initial status is `ACTIVE` (or becomes `ACTIVE` upon successful MFA verification if sensitive), and its first execution date is scheduled exactly one full interval after creation timestamp, never executing immediately at creation time (REC-04, A15, D-09).
3. **Given** a recurring payment schedule created with an explicit start date, **When** the user inputs the date in `YYYY-MM-DD` format, **Then** the system validates that the date represents a strictly future instant relative to current time (rejecting current or past UTC dates), schedules the first execution due at `00:00:00 UTC` on that date, and clearly indicates in the UI that the execution instant is interpreted in UTC (REC-04, D-09).
4. **Given** a recurring schedule creation request with periodic amount >= COP 1,000,000.00, **When** submitted, **Then** the schedule is persisted in `PENDING_MFA` status (not `ACTIVE`) with a 300-second validity window and displays the MFA code verification modal. No funds are debited at creation or confirmation time. Closing or dismissing the modal leaves the schedule in `PENDING_MFA` without cancelling it, allowing the customer to resume verification from the UI. Entering code `123456` within the window transitions the schedule to `ACTIVE` (with first due date following the established schedule without shifting). If the 300-second challenge window expires, the schedule transitions to `EXPIRED` without financial operations and cannot be executed (D-03, D-05, D-09).
5. **Given** an active schedule whose due date has arrived while the application is running, **When** the automated scheduler processes it, **Then** the payment executes automatically without human interaction or MFA prompts, debits the source account, logs the transaction, and advances the next due date by exactly one interval from the previous scheduled due date (preserving UTC execution instant) (D-09).
6. **Given** an active schedule due for execution where the source account has insufficient funds or would exceed dynamic limits, **When** automated execution is attempted, **Then** the failure is logged, account balance is not debited, and the next due date advances by one interval so the schedule does not become stuck (REC-10, A19).
7. **Given** an active schedule, **When** the customer pauses the schedule, **Then** its status becomes `PAUSED` and it is skipped by the scheduler even if its due date passes.
8. **Given** a paused schedule, **When** the customer resumes it, **Then** its status returns to `ACTIVE` and becomes eligible for execution at its next due date.
9. **Given** an active or paused schedule, **When** the customer cancels the schedule, **Then** its status transitions to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal: any attempt to resume, pause, or execute a cancelled schedule is strictly rejected.
10. **Given** a recurring ACH payment schedule created using a saved beneficiary, **When** the saved beneficiary record is subsequently deleted by the customer, **Then** the schedule retains its immutable recipient snapshot (recipient name, bank name, routing number, account number) and continues executing according to its schedule and status without modification or cancellation (REC-11, D-08).

### Edge Cases

- **Exact Limit Boundary**: What happens when a transfer amount is exactly equal to the remaining per-transfer or daily limit? The transaction MUST be permitted if balance and other validations pass (LIM-05).
- **Limit Exceeded with Valid MFA**: What happens if a customer attempts a sensitive transfer that exceeds the daily limit but enters the correct MFA code `123456`? The transaction MUST be rejected at limit check prior to completion; a valid MFA code never bypasses a limit (LIM-07, A14).
- **Daily Limit Reduced Below Current Day's Consumption**: What happens when a customer reduces the daily limit below the amount already consumed today? The update MUST be accepted immediately; previously authorized transfers remain intact; remaining daily availability becomes COP 0.00 (`max(0, daily_limit - consumed)`) rather than negative; new outgoing transfers are rejected until availability opens via an approved limit increase, valid refund, or the next UTC midnight reset; the configured limit persists across days without reverting (D-07).
- **MFA Challenge Expiration for Schedule Creation**: What happens if the customer takes longer than 300 seconds to confirm MFA when creating a recurring schedule? The pending schedule creation MUST transition to `EXPIRED` and be rejected without creating an active schedule, without executing, and without debiting funds (D-05).
- **MFA Modal Closed for Schedule Creation**: What happens if the customer closes the MFA modal while creating a sensitive recurring schedule? The schedule MUST remain persisted in `PENDING_MFA` status and appear in the UI as a pending authorization that can be resumed within its 300-second validity window, without being executed or cancelled prematurely (D-05).
- **Recurring Start Date Boundary Validation**: What happens if the customer selects today's date or a past date for a recurring schedule? The system MUST strictly reject the request with a validation error; start dates must represent a strictly future instant relative to creation time (D-09).
- **Daily Limit UTC Reset**: How is the daily limit calculated across days? Daily cumulative usage MUST be computed against a documented UTC day boundary (00:00:00Z to 23:59:59Z). Completed internal transfers and pending/settled ACH transfers count against the limit; failed/refunded ACH transfers MUST NOT count against the consumed limit (LIM-06, D-06).
- **Reprocessing / Idempotency**: What happens if an ACH settlement or failure transition is invoked multiple times for the same transfer? Duplicate transitions MUST be idempotent and MUST NOT produce double debits or double refunds; `SETTLED` and `FAILED` are mutually exclusive terminal states (ACH-08, A08, D-06).
- **Deleted Beneficiary on Active Schedule & History**: What happens if a customer deletes a saved beneficiary that is referenced by an active recurring schedule or past transfers? The schedule and all past transfers MUST retain immutable snapshot recipient data (name, bank, routing number, account number) and continue executing or displaying without alteration, without debiting funds or causing cancellation (REC-11, D-08).
- **Schedule Due During Downtime**: What happens if the backend was shut down when a recurring payment was due? When the application starts up and the scheduler runs, it MUST detect active overdue schedules and execute them or advance their due dates appropriately.
- **Non-positive Amounts & Monetary Precision**: What happens if a user submits COP 0, a negative amount, or an amount with more than two decimal places (e.g., COP 100.555)? The system MUST strictly reject the request with validation errors before any state change or transfer creation (ACH-04, LIM-09, A09).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### Dynamic Limits (LIM & Decisions D-01, D-07)
- **FR-001**: System MUST enforce a configurable per-transfer maximum limit and a cumulative daily maximum limit for the mock customer, defaulting to COP 5,000,000.00 and COP 10,000,000.00 respectively (LIM-01, D-01).
- **FR-002**: System MUST provide an interface for the mock customer to view current limit values, today's accumulated consumption, and remaining daily availability calculated as `max(0, daily_limit - eligible_daily_consumption)` (never negative), and to update both limits with immediate effect, even if the new daily limit is below current daily consumption (LIM-02, A10, D-07).
- **FR-003**: System MUST reject any transfer that exceeds the effective per-transfer limit, regardless of available account balance or MFA entry (LIM-03, A11).
- **FR-004**: System MUST reject any transfer that would cause total consumption within the current UTC day to exceed the effective daily limit, including when an immediate limit reduction causes remaining availability to become zero, without modifying or reversing previously authorized transactions (LIM-04, A12, D-07).
- **FR-005**: System MUST allow transfers whose amount is exactly equal to the remaining per-transfer limit or remaining available daily limit when all other criteria are met (LIM-05, A11, D-01).
- **FR-006**: System MUST aggregate daily limit consumption strictly within a UTC calendar day (00:00:00Z to 23:59:59Z), counting completed internal transfers, pending ACH transfers, and settled ACH transfers, while excluding failed/refunded ACH transfers (LIM-06, D-06).
- **FR-007**: System MUST validate dynamic limits before initiating or completing any step-up MFA verification; a valid MFA code MUST NEVER bypass limit checks (LIM-07, A14).
- **FR-008**: System MUST reject invalid limit configurations, including non-positive values or monetary amounts with more than two decimal places (LIM-09).

#### Simulated ACH Transfers & Beneficiaries (ACH & Decisions D-06, D-08)
- **FR-009**: System MUST allow the customer to initiate third-party ACH transfers by either selecting an existing saved beneficiary or providing ad hoc recipient information (ACH-01).
- **FR-010**: System MUST support Create, List, and Delete operations for saved beneficiaries; banking details (name, bank, routing number, account number) are immutable once created (updates require deleting and creating a new record), and saved beneficiaries belong exclusively to the authenticated mock user (ACH-02, D-08).
- **FR-011**: System MUST require and validate recipient fields for all ACH transfers: recipient name, bank name, routing number (strictly exactly 9 numeric digits), and account number (strictly 4 to 17 numeric digits) (ACH-03, A05, A06).
- **FR-012**: System MUST validate ACH routing numbers immediately in the UI before submission, disabling submission and providing real-time feedback until 9 numeric digits are entered (ACH-10, A05).
- **FR-013**: System MUST validate that ACH transfer amounts are strictly positive, have at most two decimal places, do not exceed source account balance, and satisfy dynamic limits (ACH-04, A09).
- **FR-014**: System MUST record newly accepted ACH transfers in `pending` status, debit the source account balance immediately once upon acceptance, and display the pending transfer in account history retaining an immutable recipient snapshot (ACH-05, A07, D-08).
- **FR-015**: System MUST autonomously settle eligible `pending` ACH transfers in the background, transitioning their status to `settled` with a recorded settlement timestamp without debiting the source account again (ACH-06, A07, D-06).
- **FR-016**: System MUST support transitioning an eligible `pending` ACH transfer to `failed` exclusively through an explicit non-public/test-only simulation mechanism, refunding the exact debited amount to the source account balance exactly once and excluding the transfer from UTC daily limit consumption; valid recipient fields must not trigger failures through artificial naming or numbering rules (ACH-07, A08, D-06).
- **FR-017**: System MUST ensure that `settled` and `failed` are mutually exclusive terminal states for ACH transfers, and duplicate transition requests or reprocessing attempts are idempotent and cannot cause double debits or double refunds (ACH-08, A08, D-06).
- **FR-018**: System MUST clearly differentiate internal and ACH transfers in the user interface, providing distinct forms, saved beneficiary pickers, an option to save ad hoc beneficiaries, beneficiary management controls (create, list, delete), and visible transfer lifecycle status (ACH-09, D-08).

#### Scheduled Recurring Payments (REC & Decisions D-03, D-04, D-05, D-08, D-09)
- **FR-019**: System MUST allow creating recurring payment schedules for both internal and ACH transfers with a valid source, destination, periodic amount, and supported frequency (REC-01).
- **FR-020**: System MUST support exactly three recurring frequencies: Daily (increment of 1 day / 24 hours), Weekly (increment of 7 days), and Monthly (strictly increment of 30 days, not variable calendar months) (REC-02, A16, D-09).
- **FR-021**: System MUST initialize newly created standard recurring schedules (amount < COP 1,000,000.00) in `ACTIVE` status (REC-03). Sensitive recurring schedules (amount >= COP 1,000,000.00) MUST initialize in `PENDING_MFA` status until confirmed (D-05).
- **FR-022**: When an explicit start date is supplied, system MUST require calendar format `YYYY-MM-DD`, validate that it represents a strictly future instant (rejecting current or past UTC dates), and schedule the first execution at `00:00:00 UTC` on that date; if omitted, the first execution date MUST be set to the creation timestamp plus exactly one full configured interval (never immediately on creation); MFA confirmation does not alter the established due date (REC-04, A15, D-09).
- **FR-023**: System MUST automatically process active recurring schedules when due while the application/backend is running, executing transfers without requiring interactive human intervention and advancing subsequent due dates by exactly one configured interval from the previous due date, preserving the UTC execution instant (REC-05, D-04, D-09).
- **FR-024**: System MUST allow the customer to pause active schedules, resume paused schedules, and cancel schedules (REC-06, REC-12).
- **FR-025**: System MUST treat schedule cancellation as terminal: a `CANCELLED` schedule cannot be resumed or transitioned to any other state (REC-07, A18).
- **FR-026**: System MUST ensure that `PENDING_MFA`, `PAUSED`, `EXPIRED`, and `CANCELLED` schedules are never executed by the automated scheduler; only schedules in `ACTIVE` status are eligible for execution (REC-08, D-05).
- **FR-027**: When creating a recurring payment schedule with periodic amount >= COP 1,000,000.00, system MUST persist the schedule in `PENDING_MFA` status with a 300-second validity window and require simulated MFA verification using code `123456` with unlimited retries and no lockout prior to activation, without debiting funds at creation or confirmation time; closing the modal retains `PENDING_MFA` for UI resumption, and expired challenges transition to `EXPIRED` without executing (REC-09, D-03, D-05).
- **FR-028**: Automated recurring executions MUST NOT prompt for interactive MFA, but MUST re-evaluate source account balance and dynamic limits at every execution run, respecting current limits including any recent reductions (REC-08, REC-09, LIM-08, D-07).
- **FR-029**: If an automated execution fails due to insufficient funds or exceeded dynamic limits, system MUST record the failure, leave the source balance undebited, and advance the next due date by one interval so the schedule does not become stuck (REC-10, A19).
- **FR-030**: Recurring ACH schedules MUST capture and retain an immutable snapshot of recipient banking details upon creation, such that subsequent deletion of a saved beneficiary does not invalidate, modify, or cancel the schedule (REC-11, D-08).
- **FR-031**: System MUST provide a recurring payments UI displaying all schedules, their statuses (`PENDING_MFA`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED`), periodic amounts, destinations, next due dates (indicating UTC interpretation), and controls to pause, resume, cancel, or resume pending MFA verification with clear feedback (REC-12, D-05, D-09).

#### Inherited Banking Invariants (HER & Decision D-02)
- **FR-032**: System MUST preserve all existing Stage 1 account endpoints, Checking and Savings account models, friendly names, balance queries, and transaction history views without regression (HER-01, A01).
- **FR-033**: System MUST preserve own-account internal transfers, immediate balance updates, overdraft prevention, and debit/credit history records (HER-02, A02).
- **FR-034**: System MUST preserve the inherited MFA verification threshold (amount >= COP 1,000,000.00), mock verification code `123456`, 300-second on-demand expiration window, and infinite retry policy for sensitive direct transfers (HER-03, A13).
- **FR-035**: System MUST require inherited MFA verification for direct sensitive ACH transfers (amount >= COP 1,000,000.00), executed strictly after limit validation checks pass (HER-04).

### Key Entities

- **Customer Account**: Represents the mock user's financial accounts. Key attributes: Account Identifier, Friendly Label (`Checking`, `Savings`), Current Balance (COP decimal), Currency (`COP`).
- **Dynamic Limits**: Represents the customer's transfer limit configuration. Key attributes: Per-Transfer Maximum Limit (COP decimal, default 5,000,000.00), Daily Maximum Limit (COP decimal, default 10,000,000.00), Last Updated Timestamp.
- **Beneficiary**: Represents a saved external ACH recipient belonging to the customer. Key attributes: Beneficiary Identifier, Customer Identifier, Recipient Name, Bank Name, Routing Number (9 numeric digits), Account Number (4–17 numeric digits), Created Timestamp. Details are immutable.
- **Transfer**: Represents a financial movement (internal own-account or third-party ACH). Key attributes: Transfer Identifier, Transfer Type (`INTERNAL`, `ACH`), Source Account Identifier, Destination (Internal Account ID or External Recipient Snapshot), Amount (COP decimal), Status (`COMPLETED`, `PENDING_MFA`, `PENDING`, `SETTLED`, `FAILED`, `EXPIRED`), Created Timestamp, Settlement Timestamp, Failure/Refund Timestamp.
- **Recurring Schedule**: Represents an automated payment instruction. Key attributes: Schedule Identifier, Customer Identifier, Transfer Type (`INTERNAL`, `ACH`), Source Account Identifier, Destination Details (Immutable Recipient Snapshot for ACH), Periodic Amount (COP decimal), Frequency (`DAILY`, `WEEKLY`, `MONTHLY`), Status (`PENDING_MFA`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED`), MFA Expiration Timestamp (nullable), Next Due Date (ISO-8601 UTC), Last Executed Date, Created Timestamp.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of transfer requests (internal, ACH, and recurring) are evaluated against active dynamic limits; any transfer exceeding either the per-transfer limit or remaining daily limit (including after an immediate limit reduction) is rejected before MFA step-up.
- **SC-002**: 100% of ACH routing numbers are validated for exactly 9 numeric digits and account numbers for 4–17 digits, with immediate UI feedback preventing submission of malformed inputs.
- **SC-003**: 100% of accepted ACH transfers immediately debit the source account balance once upon entering `pending` state; autonomous background settlement produces zero additional debits; failed ACH transfers simulated via the test-only mechanism refund the debited amount exactly once with complete idempotency under repeated calls.
- **SC-004**: 100% of recurring schedules adhere to exact frequency intervals: Daily (+1 day / 24 hours), Weekly (+7 days), and Monthly (strictly +30 days); newly created schedules without a start date never execute on creation day; explicit start dates strictly execute at 00:00:00 UTC on future dates.
- **SC-005**: 100% of sensitive recurring schedule creations (amount >= COP 1,000,000.00) are persisted in `PENDING_MFA` status requiring simulated MFA verification (`123456`) before transitioning to `ACTIVE`; closing the modal allows subsequent resumption within 300 seconds; 0% of automated execution cycles prompt for interactive MFA.
- **SC-006**: 100% of overdue active recurring schedules are processed automatically without manual human intervention while the application is running, advancing their due date even upon funding or limit failure.
- **SC-007**: 100% of inherited Stage 1 tests (12 backend tests, 5 frontend tests) continue to pass without regression, maintaining fresh seed balances of COP 5,000,000.00 (Checking) and COP 15,000,000.00 (Savings) in COP currency.
- **SC-008**: 100% of shared acceptance scenarios A01 through A21 are demonstrably verifiable with automated tests or observable manual criteria.
- **SC-009**: 100% of active recurring schedules and historical transfers referencing a deleted beneficiary continue executing or displaying without errors, retaining immutable destination snapshots.

## Assumptions

- **A-001**: The application operates with a single authenticated mock user session, consistent with Stage 1; multi-user authentication, registration, password management, and session timeouts remain strictly out of scope.
- **A-002**: All monetary operations are denominated in Colombian Pesos (COP) and formatted according to Colombian conventions; default limits of COP 5,000,000.00 (per-transfer) and COP 10,000,000.00 (daily) reflect approved researcher decision D-01.
- **A-003**: ACH interbank transfers and beneficiary validation are fully simulated; no external clearing house (NACHA/FedACH), live banking APIs, or real routing directory queries are performed; test-only simulation mechanisms trigger deterministic failure states without artificial recipient rules.
- **A-004**: Automated processing of due recurring schedules runs continuously in the background or on periodic scheduled evaluation while the backend service is operational; test suites may utilize an explicit test-only mechanism to trigger execution verifications without requiring real-time clock advancement.
- **A-005**: Daily limit consumption resets strictly at midnight UTC (00:00:00Z).
- **A-006**: Persistent state is retained in server-side storage across service restarts, maintaining isolated test fixtures that do not modify the preserved historical Stage 1 SQLite database.
