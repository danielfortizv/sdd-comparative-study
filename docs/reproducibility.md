# Reproducibility and Study Scope

## Original Standardization Goal

The study was originally conceived as a rigorous, professionally oriented evaluation of the material generated by the selected SDD tools. To make the comparison as consistent as practical, the experiment standardized the language model across tools that allowed model selection and used shared seed files to provide the same case- and stage-specific requirements. The tools were also used through their standard workflows, without custom skills or advanced platform-specific optimizations.

Gemini 3.5 Flash was used where the underlying model could be selected. Kiro was the exception: its closed platform does not allow users to configure the underlying model, so it was run in its default Auto mode. The seed documents, tool workflows, versions, environment, and available usage records provide traceability for the executions.

## Exploratory Interpretation

The study is an exploratory comparison, or an induction from observed cases, rather than an exact or fully reproducible experiment. Its initial goal of rigorous standardization could not remove important sources of variation:

- **LLM outputs are nondeterministic.** The same prompt, seed, and tool can produce different plans, specifications, code, and interactions on another execution.
- **The budget did not support repeated runs.** The study therefore could not estimate run-to-run variability or establish that the observed outcome is typical for a tool.
- **Kiro introduces a platform and model confound.** Its greater platform integration and robustness, combined with its use of an underlying model different from the shared configurable model and inaccessible to the researchers, means that observed differences cannot be attributed solely to the SDD method or workflow.
- **The tools expose different capabilities and evidence.** Some usage measures and workflow details are unavailable or collected through different mechanisms, limiting direct equivalence across platforms.

Consequently, the results describe the specific executions and artifacts preserved in this repository. They can support qualitative observations and exploratory comparisons, but they do not establish deterministic outcomes, statistical generalization, or causal superiority of one SDD tool. Repeating an experiment may be useful for further investigation, but an identical result should not be expected.

## Shared Change-Control Matrix

Despite these limits, the study shares a proposed change-control matrix for the four cases across all four development stages. The matrix summarizes the stage objectives and the planned instructions, scope, and requirements for each case. It is documented in the separate LaTeX file [`tabla etapas.tex`](../../../tabla%20etapas.tex) in the parent `Proyecto de Grado` directory. This matrix makes the intended progression of the cases inspectable; it does not imply that every proposed case-stage change was executed in every tool implementation.

## Traceability

Although exact reproduction is not claimed, the repository preserves evidence that allows readers to inspect how the reported observations were produced. Where available, each execution should retain:

- the case- and stage-specific seed and starting project state;
- tool, agent, model, and environment details;
- prompts, native specifications, plans, tasks, and generated or modified code;
- session records, measurements, elapsed time, and human interventions;
- test and static-analysis results, screenshots, defects, and regressions.

Preserve each execution as a distinct record and identify unavailable measurements explicitly. Do not reconstruct missing details from memory or treat a rerun as a byte-for-byte reproduction of a prior LLM output.
